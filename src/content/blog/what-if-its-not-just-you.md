We're often told to look inward when something feels off. To reflect, regulate, and work on ourselves.

And that matters. Self-awareness is important, and therapy often starts there.

But sometimes, this focus becomes so strong that we start to believe something else that if something isn't working, it must be coming from within us.

So we keep turning inward. Trying to understand why we feel the way we do. Why we react the way we do. Why certain situations seem harder than they should be.

What can get missed in all of this is context.

We don't exist in isolation. The way we feel; our anxiety, our emotional reactions, the way we show up in relationships is shaped not only by who we are, but also by where we are and who we are with.

You might notice yourself feeling anxious in meetings with a particular manager. You overthink what you say, second-guess yourself, and hold back more than you'd like. But in other professional spaces, you feel clear, confident, and engaged.

So what changed?

Not just you.

It might be the dynamic. The environment. The way you are responded to.

Or think about this: you might find yourself becoming quieter in a friendship where you often feel interrupted or overlooked. Over time, you share less, say less, and start to feel a little distant. But in other relationships, you might feel open and expressive without even thinking about it.

That shift isn't random.

Even in long-term relationships, the way we respond can slowly change based on what the relationship feels like over time. If you often feel unheard, you might start raising your voice more quickly. If you expect criticism, you might avoid certain conversations altogether. On the surface, this can look like irritability or distance. But often, it's a response to something that's been building.

This is where self-work can sometimes feel confusing.

If you're only looking inward, you might land on conclusions like, "I'm too sensitive," or "I have anxiety," or "I just need to communicate better." And while there may be some truth in that, it's not the full picture.

## What Am I Responding To Here?

There's another question that's just as important: *What am I responding to here?*

Without asking that, it becomes easy to take on responsibility for everything, to believe that with enough effort or awareness, you should be able to show up the same way in every situation, with every person.

But that's not how people work.

We are shaped by our environments. By how we are treated. By whether we feel at ease, understood, or on edge. This doesn't take away from personal responsibility, but it does add context to what you're experiencing.

At the same time, a lot of us have been taught that being independent and self-sufficient is a strength. That we should be able to manage our emotions on our own and not be too affected by others.

Over time, this can make us really good at managing ourselves but less aware of whether the environments we're in are actually working for us.

## Widening the Lens

Bringing context back into the conversation doesn't mean ignoring your role in things. It just means widening the lens a little.

To hold both: that there are parts of you that can grow and shift, and that there are also relationships and environments that shape how those parts show up.

Sometimes, understanding yourself more clearly isn't just about looking inward.

It's also about noticing where you feel most like yourself, where you don't, and what that might be telling you.

Because sometimes, you're not the problem.

You're responding to one.
